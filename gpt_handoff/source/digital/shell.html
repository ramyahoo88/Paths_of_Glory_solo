How to play

Each turn has six action rounds. Central Powers plays first in every round. The opposing computer chooses its cards, orders and combat reactions from its own hand and the public board.

  1. Choose a card. Click a card in your hand, then choose Operations, Strategic Redeployment, Replacements or its Event. The 1 OP order keeps your hand intact.
  2. Issue orders. For Operations, select spaces on the map and assign Move or Attack. Confirm activations before moving. Select counters to move together, then choose a destination. For combat, select a target and choose its attacking units.
  3. Resolve combat. The game rolls dice. Select your own eligible combat cards, losses, retreats and advances when prompted, or use the suggested order. You can enable automatic combat reactions when you start a game.
  4. Keep supply open. Orange unit markers indicate broken supply. Reconnect the route before turn-end attrition. Ports, forts and dashed national connections affect supply.
  5. Check the score. Allies win at 10 VP or fewer, Central Powers at 14 VP or more, and 11–13 VP is a draw. The game also checks for automatic victory at 0 or 20 VP at the War Status Phase.

Save and resume

The app attempts to autosave in this browser. Use Save for a portable JSON file and Load to resume from it. On the map, drag to pan and scroll or use +/− to zoom.

Central Powers AI

In the early rounds of August and September, the AI can preview an Allied reply and its own next action. The War diary shows a possible follow-up when the AI selects one. The AI checks the board again after the actual Allied play.

Rules and credits

This independent fan implementation covers the three-turn Introductory Scenario with the classic setup and Mobilization decks. Ted Raicer designed Paths of Glory, published by GMT Games. The map and interface are original schematics. The app uses rule, card and board facts from the references below.
